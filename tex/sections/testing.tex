\section{How the Claims are Tested}\label{sec:testing}
Every claim in \cite{shwartzziv2017} is read off the trajectory of one
architecture in the information plane. In \cite{saxe2018} each claim is
instead tested by varying one component at a time whilst the rest is held
fixed.

Both the estimator and the nonlinearity are treated as variables rather than
as given, as covered in \autoref{subsec:estimating}. The same networks are
measured by binning, by kernel density and by $k$-nearest-neighbours, so that
no claim rests on one discretization, and the minimal model puts the
compression on an analytical footing where $I(X;T)$ follows exactly from the
weight. The phenomenon is therefore a mechanism and not an observation.

The optimizer is isolated in the same manner. Training with full batch
gradient descent removes the stochasticity whilst everything else is held
fixed, which is what makes the claim of \cite{shwartzziv2017} falsifiable to
begin with.

Finally the data is constructed such that the task-irrelevant dimensions are
known beforehand. Consequently they can ask what compresses, and not merely
whether the measured quantity falls.

These are the methodological improvements over \cite{shwartzziv2017}, where a
single architecture, binning scheme and optimizer are used throughout, and
every claim consequently follows from the same experiment.

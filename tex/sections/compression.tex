\section{The Causes of the Observed Compression}\label{sec:compression}
A rebuttal paper released shortly after \cite{shwartzziv2017} written by Saxe et
al. \cite{saxe2018} will be the second main focus of this paper.
They provide evidence that several claims from the former paper
do not hold in the general case, and that they misinterpret the role of SGD.
This is not loosely related but a direct result of the initial paper.
Here we will focus our attention on what has a direct causal link to
the observed compression phenomenon as seen in the information plane, and
measured by the mutual information $I(X; T)$.

\subsection{Estimating the mutual information}\label{subsec:estimating}
The binning of \cite{shwartzziv2017} discretizes the hidden activity $h$ into
the variable $T=\mathrm{bin}(h)$. Since $T$ is a deterministic function of $X$
the conditional entropy $H(T\mid X)$ vanishes, and therefore
\begin{equation}
	I(X;T) = H(T)-H(T\mid X) = H(T) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_i\log p_i
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ is the probability that an input lands in bin $i$.

To show what this measures they consider a minimal model of a single unit
$h=f(w_1X)$ with $X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, in which $p_i$ is found exactly from
the cumulative density of $X$ for any monotonic nonlinearity $f$,
\begin{equation}
	p_i = P\left(f^{-1}(b_i)/w_1 \leq X < f^{-1}(b_{i+1})/w_1\right)
\end{equation}
with $b_i$ and $b_{i+1}$ the limits of bin $i$. As a function of the weight
$w_1$ the entropy $H(T)$ first rises and then falls for tanh, whilst it only
rises for ReLU. Consequently the measured information is a property of the
bins as much as of the network.

Subsequently they re-estimate $I(X;T)$ with the kernel density method of
\cite{kolchinsky2017}, which treats the hidden activity as a mixture of
Gaussians by adding noise $T=h+\epsilon$ with
$\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2I)$, giving the upper bound
\begin{equation}
	I(X;T) \leq -\frac{1}{P}\sum_i\log\frac{1}{P}\sum_j
	\exp\left(-\frac{\lVert h_i-h_j\rVert_2^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
\end{equation}
over the $P$ samples, and with the $k$-nearest-neighbour estimator of
\cite{kraskov2004} as a further check. The noise is added for the analysis
alone and is present in neither training nor testing. Neither the binning nor
the noise is therefore a part of the network, and different choices of either
give different values of the measured information.

\subsection{Revisiting the claims of the original paper}
In \cite{shwartzziv2017} they claim that the stochasticity introduced by SGD
induces a random diffusion process which can be described by the Fokker-Planck
equation. Their argument is that this diffusion, subject to the error
constraint, maximizes the conditional entropy $H(X\mid T_i)$ i.e. minimizes the
mutual information $I(X;T_i)=H(X)-H(X\mid T_i)$, since $H(X)$ is fixed by the
data. Thus attributing the compression to the optimization method SGD. In
\cite{saxe2018} they show that compression is seen as a result of:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Double-saturating nonlinearities} cause the hidden activations
	to saturate as training progresses. The binned activity then collapses into
	the two extreme bins, and the measured $I(X;T)$ drops. Their tanh network
	compresses, their ReLU network does not. The same holds when the mutual
	information is estimated with kernel density and $k$-nearest-neighbour
	estimators instead of binning.
	\item \textbf{Growth in weight magnitude} makes the units enter this
	saturated regime in the first place, as their minimal model shows.
	\item \textbf{Genuinely task-irrelevant input dimensions} do compress, but
	this happens at the same time as the fitting and not in a separate phase
	afterwards. The information on the input as a whole can still increase.
\end{itemize}

Subsequently they find that compression is \textit{not} a result of:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{The drift-diffusion transition} in the gradient SNR. They find
	this transition in all cases, also in networks that never compress, so the
	two phases are not causally related to the compression.
	\item \textbf{The maximum entropy argument} of \cite{shwartzziv2017}, which
	they argue does not hold theoretically. The stationary distribution it
	describes is a distribution over weights across different training runs,
	whilst $H(X\mid T)$ is about the inputs, and this gives no reason that the
	weights found in one particular run maximize $H(X\mid T)$. They do not show
	the claim to be false, but leave the burden of proof with
	\cite{shwartzziv2017}.
	\item \textbf{The stochasticity of SGD}, as they train the same networks
	with full batch gradient descent, which has no randomness in its updates,
	and still see comparable compression in the tanh networks.
\end{itemize}
where their explanation thus reduces to a single mechanism; the weights grow
during training, and the double-saturating nonlinearity turns this growth into
a reduction in the measured $I(X;T)$.
